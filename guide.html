How the QR code and the link work

A QR code is just a web address drawn as a pattern of squares. The phone's camera reads the pattern and opens the address. Nothing else is inside the code. No customer details, nothing that can run out or expire.

The code

The address

The page

The form, loaded from that address.

What the address means

chiranr19.github.io/bullseye-intake/?ref=flyer
  • chiranr19.github.io — where the page lives. A free web space that Chiran looks after.
  • bullseye-intake — the name of your form on that space.
  • ?ref=flyer — which code was scanned. It only fills the Source column in the spreadsheet. Change the last word and you get the other three codes.

Where the page lives, and why there

The page sits on GitHub Pages. GitHub is a large, well-known service where people keep the files behind websites and software. It hosts simple pages like this one for free, with no ads, no monthly bill and no "your trial has ended". It has been around since 2008 and runs on a fast network, so the page opens quickly on one bar of signal. Chiran keeps the files there and can fix or change the page from his laptop.

The address is fixed. If it ever had to move, every printed QR code would need reprinting, so it won't move without telling you first.

Where the details go

The page itself keeps nothing. The moment a customer taps Send, their details travel straight into a Google account: the email to Gmail, the row to Google Sheets, the photos to Google Drive. Google is the same company that already runs most people's email, and this uses the free part of it. While we set things up they go to Chiran's account. Once we switch it to your Gmail, they go to yours and Chiran sees nothing.

Is it safe?

  • The padlock. The address starts with https. Everything the customer types is scrambled between their phone and Google, so nobody on a shop's Wi-Fi can read it on the way.
  • Only you see it. The spreadsheet, the folders and the emails are private to the Google account. Nothing is published anywhere. The page is also marked so search engines don't list it.
  • Nothing is sold, tracked or shared. No ads, no cookies, no tracking, no outside company in the middle.
  • One outside lookup, and only for the VIN. When a customer types a VIN, the page asks the U.S. government's free vehicle database (NHTSA) for the year, make and model. It sends the VIN and nothing else. If that lookup is slow or down, the customer just types the car in by hand.
  • Robots are kept out. A hidden trap field, a timer, and a code word baked into the page stop spam programs. Junk is thrown away before it reaches your spreadsheet.
  • Photos are shrunk first. The customer's phone shrinks the card photo before sending, so it goes through fast and doesn't fill up your Drive. The text on the card stays readable.
  • The signature means what the paper one means. The customer signs under the same wording as your paper form. The page saves the signature as a picture, plus the time and which version of the wording they saw.
  • If something is down. On the rare day Google or GitHub has a problem, the customer sees "Couldn't send" and a button that opens a text to you with their details instead. Paper still works too.

Good to know
